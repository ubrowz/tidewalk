How it works

  • Today: every morning the tide is out and the water is hazy. Each step raises it and clears it; at your goal it is high tide with clear water.
  • Good days: reaching your goal brings gifts: a feeding, a visitor (a sea turtle, an eagle ray or a barracuda), and a youngster for one of the reef's couples.
  • Weeks: as good days add up, new species move in and coral grows on the reef floor. Fish pair up, raise young and live a couple of weeks, so the reef has generations.
  • Pollution: on a day you don't reach your goal, a piece of pollution may drift into the reef: a bottle, a can, a tyre, a plastic bag or an old fishing net. On a day you reach your goal, tap one piece to clear it. If you don't, it is gone by the next morning. The fish are never harmed by it.
  • Fish Challenges: walk for the fish you want. Pick one, take on its walking challenge, and a pair moves in when you've done it.

Common questions

The app doesn't show my steps

Check that Tidewalk may read your steps: Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Tidewalk, and turn on Steps. Steps from a watch only appear once the watch has synced with the Health app.

My watch isn't an Apple Watch

Tidewalk reads steps from Apple Health. Most watches (for example Garmin) can sync their steps to Apple Health in their own app's settings. Without any steps in Health, Tidewalk uses the iPhone's own step count.

How do I change my daily step goal?

Tap the water drop in the top corner of the screen. The sheet that opens shows today's steps and lets you change your goal.

How do I turn the sound off?

Tap the speaker at the top of the screen. The sound also follows your iPhone's silent switch.

How do Fish Challenges work?

Tap the fish button at the top of the screen, between the water drop and the speaker. Pick a fish to see its challenge, for example reaching your goal on three days, and tap Walk for this fish. A ring on the button shows how far you are; when you're done, a pair moves into your reef. There is nothing to buy: you only need your steps. You can walk for one fish at a time, and there is no deadline.

Can I see my tide without opening the app?

Yes, with the Tidewalk widget. Hold your finger on an empty spot of your Home Screen, tap Edit → Add Widget (or +), search for Tidewalk and add Today's tide. It also fits on the Lock Screen. The widget shows what Tidewalk last read: with Apple Health connected it keeps up by itself; with the iPhone's own step count it updates when you open the app.

Can I play with the fish?

Tap a fish to bring it to the front and see its card: its name (which you can change), its age, its partner and its young. Hold a finger in open water and a curious fish comes to nibble at it. Double-tap anywhere to knock on the glass: the fish dart away for a moment, and the clownfish hide in their anemone. Swipe sideways to follow the fish along the reef: it is wider than your screen.

Can I look at the reef from above?

Yes. Tap the view button at the top of the screen (left of the speaker): the camera glides up and you look down into the reef, with the fish swimming over the sand and coral. Tap it again to look through the glass. Tidewalk remembers which view you like.

Why does the reef turn with my phone?

The water stays level, like in a real tank, so the reef turns when you tilt your iPhone. Looking down from above, it stays still.

Why is there pollution in my reef?

Real reefs suffer from plastic, glass and lost fishing nets. In Tidewalk a piece drifts in on a day you don't reach your goal, and there are never more than ten. On a day you reach your goal you can tap one away. To learn about cleaning up the real ocean, visit The Ocean Cleanup.

Can I suggest a fish for the reef?

Yes, please do. Tap Suggest a fish at the bottom of the settings or of Fish Challenges, or email jx7442soy@mozmail.com. The fish that most people ask for will be added in a later version.

Does Tidewalk collect my data?

No. Your steps and your reef stay on your iPhone. See the privacy policy.
